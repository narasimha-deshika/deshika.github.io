Nadai reference

1 = 1 maatra per beat, 2 = 2, 3 = 3 (Tishra), 4 = 4 (Chatustra), 5 = 5 (Khanda), 6 = 6 (Tishra Melkalam), 7 = 7 (Mishra), 8 = 8 (Chatushra Melkalam), 9 = 9 (Sankeerna), 10 = 10 maatras per beat.

How the Korvai Generator builds patterns

Pattern: P1 (k1) P2 (k1) P3 (k1) P4 (k1) + U1 (k2) U2 (k2) U3 = total maatras. Every Poorvangam part is followed by k1. Poorvangam (P) has 3 to 5 parts that follow a Shrothovaha (rising) or Gopuccha (falling) Yati, or are equal; neighbouring parts differ by at most 6. Uttarangam (U) has 3 parts written with the digits 5 to 10: a part such as 15 is written 555 (5 × 3). Karvai k1 is 0 or between 2 and 12, k2 is between 0 and 12. About 1 in 10 korvais adds a Madhyangam between P and U: a series (5 to 10) repeated with x reducing, for example series 5 with x = 3, 2 gives 3 3 3 3 3 + 2 2 2 2 2. Its Uttarangam must start with the series number, and the Poorvangam can be absent. P and U are never equal. Every click on Generate Korvai gives a fresh set.

Asymmetric korvais are used when the thalam's beats are divisible by 3 but the Eduppu is not (for example Rupakam with Eduppu 2), because three identical repetitions cannot land on Samam. The Uttarangam is the same in all three repetitions, while the Poorvangam follows a Gopuccha series 4d, 3d, 2d, d, 0 with k1 (2 to 12) after every part: repetition 1 plays the first 3 parts, repetition 2 the first 4 and repetition 3 all 5 (the last is a bare k1). The grand total of the three repetitions equals whole cycles plus the Eduppu, for example Rupakam with Eduppu 2: 98 = 30 + 33 + 35.
